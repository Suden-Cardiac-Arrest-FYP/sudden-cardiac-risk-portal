🫀 About Sudden Cardiac Arrest

❓

What is Sudden Cardiac Arrest?

Sudden Cardiac Arrest (SCA) is a life-threatening emergency that occurs when the heart suddenly stops beating effectively. Unlike a heart attack, which is caused by blocked blood flow, SCA is an electrical problem where the heart's rhythm becomes chaotic, causing it to quiver instead of pumping blood. Without immediate treatment, SCA leads to death within minutes.


Critical Statistics: Every year, 350,000+ Americans experience SCA outside of hospitals. The survival rate is only 9% without immediate CPR, but increases to 70% with proper intervention.

🎯

Our Mission: Prevention Through Awareness

Why Young Adults Are at Risk: Approximately 25% of SCA cases occur in people under 35. Young adults face unique risks including undiagnosed genetic conditions, intense athletic activities, substance use, and lifestyle factors. Many underlying heart conditions remain silent until a sudden cardiac event occurs.


Our Goal: Up to 70% of SCA cases are preventable through proper screening, lifestyle modifications, and early intervention. This platform empowers young adults to take control of their cardiac health and save lives through prevention.

🫀 SCA Awareness

⚠️

Warning Signs

Chest pain, shortness of breath, dizziness, fainting, rapid heartbeat, fatigue during exercise, family history of sudden cardiac death

⚡

Risk Factors

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, long QT syndrome, excessive caffeine, performance-enhancing drugs, intense physical activity, genetic disorders

🛡️

Prevention Keys

Regular cardiac screening, healthy lifestyle, stress management, avoiding stimulants, proper hydration, gradual exercise progression

📊 Risk Level Actions

Low Risk 0-25%
  • Continue healthy lifestyle habits
  • Annual health check-ups
  • Regular moderate exercise
  • Stay hydrated during activities
Moderate Risk 25-50%
  • Consult cardiologist within 3 months
  • Consider ECG and echocardiogram
  • Modify exercise intensity
  • Monitor symptoms closely
  • Reduce stress and caffeine intake
High Risk 50-75%
  • URGENT: See cardiologist within 2 weeks
  • Complete cardiac evaluation required
  • Avoid intense physical activity
  • Consider medication/intervention
  • Family screening recommended
Critical Risk 75-100%
  • EMERGENCY: See cardiologist immediately
  • Comprehensive cardiac testing
  • Restrict all strenuous activities
  • Consider ICD placement
  • Immediate lifestyle modifications

💡 Prevention Tips

🏃
Gradual Exercise: Build intensity slowly, warm up properly
💧
Stay Hydrated: Drink plenty of water, especially during exercise
☕
Limit Stimulants: Avoid excessive caffeine and energy drinks
😌
Manage Stress: Practice meditation, yoga, adequate sleep
🩺
Regular Screening: Annual ECG for high-risk individuals
